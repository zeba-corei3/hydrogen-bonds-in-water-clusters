## 1. Hydrogen Bonds in Water Clusters
**Water clusters** are groups of water molecules ($(H<sub>2O)</sub>n$) held together by hydrogen bonds.

Studying these clusters is a foundational step toward understanding the molecular-scale properties of bulk liquid and solid water, whose behavior is largely governed by extended hydrogen-bonding networks and soft vibrational modes.

As cluster size ($n$) grows, the number of possible structural isomers (local minima on the potential-energy hypersurface) increases rapidly, shifting from small cyclic arrangements ($n=3\text{–}5$) to transition and three-dimensional geometries ($n \ge 6$), such as cages, prisms, cuboids, and fused rings.


### Formation and Bonding Characteristics
In a hydrogen-bonded water cluster, a hydrogen atom covalently bonded to one oxygen atom interacts with a neighboring oxygen atom.

### Importance in Chemistry
**Atmospheric & Environmental Chemistry:** Hydrogen bonding in water clusters underlies cloud and ice formation.

**Solution Chemistry & Hydration:** It governs solvation structures and hydration shells around solutes and ions.

**Biochemistry:** Hydrogen-bonded water networks play a major role in biochemical processes, including biomolecular solvation and interactions involving proteins and DNA.

**Reactivity & Molecular Entrapment:** Differences in molecular electrostatic potential (MESP) across cluster geometries influence reactivity toward electrophiles or metal ions, and cage-like structures can be relevant to molecular entrapment.
